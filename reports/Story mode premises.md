# Story mode: three premises

The story mode is a single-player campaign that gives StickyMan a written identity on top of
"draw to fight." Each premise below comes with example levels, enemies, bosses, the jokes you
collect when you lose, and how the narrator sounds. All three keep the same core: a ninja doodle
who fights with punches, kicks and drawn walls and boulders.

**Rules every premise follows:**

- **Losing is a joke, not a punishment.** Every different way to lose is its own short gag, kept
  in a "Fails" gallery, so players try silly things on purpose.
- **Short levels.** One page is one level of 2 to 4 minutes: a few fights, one drawing puzzle,
  sometimes a boss.
- **Drawing has a story reason.** The world is paper, so drawing is how you change it.
- **Not Animator vs. Animation.** The person who drew you is never the villain, and there is no
  mouse cursor enemy.

---

## A. Margin Ninja (recommended)

**In one line:** A bored student doodles a ninja in the margin of their school notebook, and
you have to survive every class until the final bell.

**Why it works:** Everyone has doodled in class. It matches the notebook look we already have,
the eraser hazard and the red-pen colors. School humor works for the young players who fill Poki
and CrazyGames.

**Who is who:**
- **You:** the doodle ninja.
- **Sam, the student:** not seen, only heard through handwritten notes in the margin. Sam is on
  your side (Sam drew you, after all) but gets distracted easily.
- **The villains:** the school day itself, and the teacher's red pen.

**Levels (one class per page):**

| Page | Level | What happens | Boss |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Math | Numbers jump off the homework and attack. Division signs split in two when you hit them. | **Long Division**, who keeps dividing into smaller copies |
| 2 | Biology | The frog diagram comes alive. Draw walls to keep the labeled organs from escaping. | **The Frog**, who eats your boulders |
| 3 | Art | Sam's failed drawings attack: a horse with five legs, a hand with seven fingers, a sun with sunglasses. | **The Self-Portrait**, which looks nothing like Sam |
| 4 | History | Tiny doodled armies from the textbook margins. Draw castle walls to hold them off. | **A general made of dates you forgot** |
| 5 | Lunch | The page is under a lunch tray. Ketchup floods, and crumbs roll like boulders. | **The Juice Box**, which squirts |
| 6 | The pop quiz | The teacher notices. The red pen comes down from the sky. | **The Red Pen**: draws X marks that cut the page, stamps F grades that crush you |

**Ways to lose (collectibles):**
- **Graded F:** the red pen stamps you flat.
- **Shaded in:** Sam gets bored and colors you in until you're a black blob.
- **Spitball:** your page gets torn off and chewed.
- **Margin of error:** you fall off the edge of the page.
- **Fixed:** Sam erases you to "draw you better" and you come back with huge muscles and tiny legs.
- **Lost in the fold:** you get stuck in the spiral binding.
- **Confiscated:** the teacher takes the notebook. Game over, see you after school.

**The narrator (Sam's margin notes):**
- "ok he's actually good at this??"
- "pls win, there's 4 minutes left in class"
- "I can't draw hands so he doesn't get hands. deal with it"
- After a loss: "that's fine. I'll draw another one."

**Pros:** Fits our current art and mechanics best, cheapest to make, funny for any age.
**Cons:** School is a familiar setting, so the jokes must be good to stand out.

## B. The Rejected Sketch

**In one line:** You were the hero of a comic, until the artist replaced you with a shiny new
ninja. You crawl out of the trash can to win your comic back.

**Why it works:** It's a sharp joke about reboots and "make it cooler" redesigns. The new hero
is a perfect target: forty belts, sixty pouches, a cape, three swords and sunglasses. You are
just lines, so you fight with lines.

**Who is who:**
- **You:** the original simple sketch, crumpled but proud.
- **The artist:** never seen, always tired, drinking coffee.
- **The villains:** the over-designed new hero, and the editor's sticky notes with comments like
  "MAKE HIM COOLER" and "ADD MORE POUCHES," which come alive and attack.

**Levels:**

| Page | Level | What happens | Boss |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The trash can | Fight the other rejected sketches: a dragon that looks like a dog, a car with square wheels. | **The Crumpled Ball**, which rolls at you |
| 2 | The desk | Pencil shavings, coffee rings, a ruler bridge you have to draw yourself. | **The Coffee Mug**, which floods the desk |
| 3 | The storyboard | Jump between comic panels. Speech bubbles are platforms. | **The Speech Bubble** that won't stop talking |
| 4 | The editor's notes | The sticky notes attack with bad advice. | **"Make Him Cooler,"** a note that keeps adding armor to itself until it can't move |
| 5 | The final panel | The cover page. | **The New Hero**, who drops a pouch every time you hit him |

**Ways to lose (collectibles):**
- **Retconned:** you never existed, the page goes blank.
- **Cut for pacing.**
- **Background extra:** you're redrawn as a tiny figure in a crowd.
- **Merchandised:** you become a cereal box mascot.
- **Sequel bait:** "To be continued..." and the screen cuts off.
- **Redesigned:** you get your own forty belts.

**The narrator (the artist's notes on the page):**
- "why did I ever draw this guy"
- "ok fine, he's kind of iconic"
- "note to self: stop drinking coffee over the drawings"

**Pros:** The strongest satire and the most quotable jokes. A clear underdog story. Good for an
older audience on Steam.
**Cons:** Needs more new art (desk, trash can, comic panels). The jokes about comics and
redesigns land better with teens and adults than with young kids.

## C. Sticky Notes

**In one line:** "StickyMan," literally: a ninja doodled on a yellow sticky note in an office,
jumping from note to note across a busy desk.

**Why it works:** It's a pun on the game's name. Every sticky note is a small arena, and drawing
on them is natural. Office humor is easy to write.

**Who is who:**
- **You:** the doodle on note number one.
- **The office worker:** never seen; their to-do list is the level list.
- **The villains:** office supplies and meetings.

**Levels:** the monitor's edge, the stapler (**The Stapler** boss pins you down), the printer
jam, the meeting ("this fight could have been an email": the boss is a slideshow that never
ends), the shredder.

**Ways to lose (collectibles):**
- **Filed under misc.**
- **Laminated.**
- **Replied all.**
- **Shredded.**
- **Promoted to manager:** the worst ending.

**Pros:** A great name pun and very quotable office jokes.
**Cons:** Too close to *Stick It to the Stickman*, a well-known stickman game about office life.
Office humor also doesn't reach young players well. **I'd keep its best jokes as a bonus level,
not the main premise.**

## Comparison

| | A. Margin Ninja | B. Rejected Sketch | C. Sticky Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fits our current look | Best (notebook, eraser, red pen) | Good | Needs a new look |
| Uses drawing well | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Age range | All ages | Teens and adults | Adults |
| Fresh compared with other games | Good, if the jokes are good | Best | Risky (close to *Stick It to the Stickman*) |
| New art needed | Least | More | More |
| Best for | Web portals and mobile | Steam | A bonus level |

## Recommendation

Go with **A. Margin Ninja**. It turns everything we already built into a reason (the notebook,
the eraser, the ink), and it works for the young audience on web portals. Borrow B's best idea
for the Art level: Sam's failed drawings (the five-legged horse, the seven-fingered hand) as
enemies.

**How to test it cheaply:** build only **Page 1, Math class**, as a vertical slice: three short
fights, one drawing puzzle, the Long Division boss, five fail gags and Sam's notes. That's about
a week. Show it to the same testers and watch two things: do they laugh, and do they want to see
the next page?
